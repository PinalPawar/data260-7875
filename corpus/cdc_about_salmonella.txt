Title: CDC: About Salmonella

CDC estimates Salmonella causes about 1.35 million infections in the United States
every year, along with 26,500 hospitalizations and 420 deaths. Most people develop diarrhea,
fever, and stomach cramps 6 hours to 6 days after infection, and illness usually lasts 4 to 7
days without treatment. Salmonella spreads through contaminated food (especially poultry,
eggs, beef, and produce), contact with animals, and contaminated water. Diagnosis is by stool
or blood culture. Most people recover on their own with rest and fluids; severe cases may
need antibiotics or hospitalization. Prevention centers on cooking food to safe temperatures,
avoiding cross-contamination between raw and ready-to-eat foods, and washing hands after
contact with animals.
