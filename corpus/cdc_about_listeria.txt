Title: CDC: About Listeria

Listeria are bacteria (germs) that can contaminate many foods. The infection is rare
but serious, though preventable through proper food safety practices.

At-Risk Populations: pregnant women, newborns, adults 65 and older, and immunocompromised
individuals face the highest risk. Other people can be infected with Listeria, but they
rarely become seriously ill.

Health Impact Statistics: the CDC estimates that annually in the United States, approximately
1,250 individuals contract Listeria, with roughly 172 deaths occurring. The infection
represents the fourth leading cause of death from foodborne illness nationally.

Illness Types: Listeria causes two categories, invasive illness and intestinal illness, each
presenting different symptoms depending on the infected person and affected body area.

Diagnosis and Treatment: laboratory testing identifies infection by growing Listeria from
body fluid or tissue samples. Treatment approaches vary -- those with intestinal illness
typically recover without antibiotics, while invasive illness cases require antibiotic
therapy. Dehydration prevention through fluid intake is recommended for diarrhea cases.

Prevention Strategies: know contamination-prone foods, follow food safety guidelines (Clean,
Separate, Cook, Chill), and monitor foodborne outbreak alerts and recalls through official
channels.
