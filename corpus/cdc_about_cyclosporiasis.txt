Title: CDC: About Cyclosporiasis

Cyclosporiasis is caused by a microscopic parasite, Cyclospora cayetanensis. It
spreads through contaminated food or water, most often fresh produce such as leafy greens,
herbs, and berries. Symptoms typically appear about a week after exposure and include watery
diarrhea, loss of appetite, weight loss, stomach cramps, bloating, increased gas, nausea, and
fatigue. Most people recover without treatment, though antibiotics (trimethoprim/
sulfamethoxazole) can help. Prevention focuses on washing produce thoroughly and following
safe food handling practices, since the parasite is resistant to many common disinfectants.
